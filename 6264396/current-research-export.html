Economic interpretation

Domestic tourism can distribute spending regionally and reach a broad visitor base. Inbound tourism earns foreign exchange and can generate higher per-visit receipts, but imports and repatriated profits can reduce local value. Higher spending does not automatically mean higher GDP impact; volume, value added, jobs, and regional linkages matter.

03 / Seasonality, demand & pricing

Transparent by design. Monthly occupancy and normalized price indices are a hypothetical Goa scenario.

Short research report

Tourism demand varies with climate, school holidays, festivals, road access, and local attractions. Goa's winter weather can support peak beach demand, while Jaipur's heat and Manali's weather-dependent roads illustrate different seasonal mechanisms. No single seasonal calendar applies to every destination.

When demand rises but hotel and transport capacity cannot expand quickly, rates often increase. Limited inventory raises the opportunity cost of unused capacity during peak travel dates. In shoulder and low seasons, discounts, packages, and longer stays can attract flexible guests, but prices should cover marginal costs.

Seasonal peaks can improve revenue while creating congestion, environmental pressure, and temporary employment. Spreading events outside peak periods may reduce volatility. The chart is an assumed seasonal profile, not evidence of causal price elasticity.

Electronic visas and easier entry

What makes people
want to visit?

Demand depends on price and non-price drivers. A driver lever shifts the demand curve; a price change moves along it.

Festivals & events / +0%

Festivals bring concentrated demand and can increase willingness to pay. Capacity pressure around event dates creates a different market from a normal weekday.

Heritage attractions / +0%

Monuments and heritage create destination identity. Conservation, visitor access, and interpretation affect the quality and value of the experience.

Favorable climate / +0%

Weather supports the comfort and timing of travel. This positive demand lever simplifies destination-specific and seasonal effects; unfavorable climate is outside this scenario.

Culture & experiences / +0%

Food, crafts, local traditions, and cultural events enrich the visitor experience. This assumed uplift represents interest, not observed visitor spending.

Demand equation & interpretation

QD = 2,600 x (1 + total driver uplift) - 0.2P. Current total uplift: 0.0%. The demand intercept is 2,600 room-nights at zero price. The price coefficient is an assumed constant slope.

Limits to the demand-side model

Income, competing destinations, trip length, and visitor composition are excluded. Additive drivers do not capture interactions: a festival during a hot season may have less effect than the same event in pleasant weather.

For empirical work, collect booking prices, actual demand, event dates, weather, visitor income, and substitute destination prices. Report the uncertainty of any estimated response.

Can the destination
meet the demand?

Supply depends on usable capacity. A room is tangible, but transport, staffing, and services make it accessible and bookable.

Hotels / 1,000 rooms

Formal room inventory is the core accommodation supply. Daily room-night potential is different from unique visitors or occupied rooms.

Homestays / 400 rooms

Homestays diversify supply and may retain more income locally. They face host availability, licensing, standards, and seasonality constraints.

Transport / 100 index

Road access, airports, public transport, and last-mile connectivity influence whether accommodation can be reached and utilized.

Services / 100 index

Guides, food, utilities, staffing, and booking infrastructure turn physical inventory into a usable visitor experience.

Supply equation & elasticity

QS = capacity x [0.55 + 0.45 x (P / 6,000)]. Effective capacity is 1,400 room-nights. Demand elasticity is E = -0.2P/Q, or -0.86 at equilibrium.

A 10% price rise reduces quantity demanded by 8.6% in a local approximation, with drivers held constant. Supply elasticity at equilibrium is 0.45.

What this model cannot answer

It does not estimate destination-wide welfare, jobs, tourism GDP, or environmental impact. Capacity constraints, product quality, dynamic bookings, and cross-price effects would require a richer model and real observations.
