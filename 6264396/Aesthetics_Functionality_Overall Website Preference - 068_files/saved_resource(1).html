01.2 / Who moves the market

Two markets.
Different value.

Domestic travel gives the industry its broad base. International visitors bring foreign exchange and often a higher spend per journey. These are complementary, not interchangeable, demand streams.

~84% domestic visitor spend~16% international
Domestic / volume and resilience

~₹14.6 lakh cr

Spending close to home

Domestic trips support year-round hotels, road and rail travel, restaurants and small businesses across many states. A broad resident market can soften a fall in overseas arrivals, though spending per trip varies widely.

International / foreign exchange

~₹2.7 lakh cr

Spending from abroad

Foreign visitors support guides, premium stays, aviation and export-like foreign-exchange receipts. This market is more sensitive to visa access, international connectivity, exchange rates and global shocks.

The 2023 spending amounts and split are rounded teaching approximations informed by WTTC visitor-spend reporting, not an audited India GDP decomposition. They describe spending, not value added; do not add them to the tourism GDP figure above.

01.3 / Short report

The calendar
sets the price.

Tourism demand is uneven. Jaipur's cooler October-March weather and winter festivals lift heritage visits, while summer heat can reduce city breaks. The opposite pattern benefits hill destinations in April-June; monsoon travel creates a different shoulder season for nature and wellness destinations.

When peak demand runs into a fixed stock of rooms, seats and guides, hotel rates and airfares rise. In slower weeks, discounts and packages stimulate bookings but may lower revenue per room. Festivals can create brief price spikes even outside the usual peak. Staggered events, better all-season experiences and flexible staffing help spread income and protect jobs.

02.3 / Policy evaluation

Policy changes
the curve.

Government action can reduce the cost of travel, grow destination capacity or make a place more visible. The economic gain depends on execution, crowding and whether benefits reach local firms.

01

Visa access

India's e-Visa system reduces application friction for eligible foreign visitors. Easier entry can lift arrivals, length of stay and foreign-exchange receipts, but air capacity and on-ground experience still constrain the result.

Evaluate with

Eligible-market arrivals, conversion to trips, nights stayed and spend per arrival before and after a reform.

02

Investment & subsidies

Swadesh Darshan 2.0 and PRASHAD support destination amenities; state tourism subsidies or hotel incentives can lower private fixed costs. Better facilities shift usable supply outward, but poorly targeted support risks idle assets and fiscal cost.

Evaluate with

Occupancy, private investment crowded in, local jobs, upkeep costs and benefits per public rupee.

03

Destination marketing

Incredible India and state campaigns can shift demand toward lesser-known places and shoulder seasons. Marketing raises awareness, but its payoff depends on authentic experiences and sufficient capacity.

Evaluate with

Search-to-booking conversion, off-season visits, visitor yield and repeat travel, not impressions alone.

These are economic mechanisms, not causal impact estimates. A credible evaluation compares similar destinations or traveler markets over time and accounts for exchange rates, air capacity, safety and seasonality.

Section C / Hands-on project

Jaipur, as a
living market.

A three-page mini economic model. Read the drivers, explore the capacity, then change a trip price to see demand and supply respond.

Page 01 / 03The demand side

01 / Why they come

Desire becomes demand.

Demand is the number of visitor trips people are willing and able to make at a given all-in trip price. The city's appeal shifts the whole demand curve; price changes move visitors along it.

Qd = (30 + D) - 0.0025PQd in lakh trips / P in rupees per trip / D is an appeal shift
01

Festivals

Event dates create short bursts of willingness to travel and pay, especially when dates are fixed.

02

Heritage

Forts, palaces and museums are enduring reasons to choose a Jaipur trip over a substitute destination.

03

Climate

Comfortable winter weather moves city-break demand outward; extreme summer heat can move it inward.

04

Culture

Food, crafts and performing arts deepen the experience and can extend the length of stay.

Page 02 / 03The supply side

02 / What can be served

Capacity makes it possible.

Supply is the number of trips the destination can serve at a given price. More rooms, reliable transport and quality services shift the supply curve outward. In the short run, fixed capacity limits the response.

Qs = (5 + S) + 0.00375PQs in lakh trips / P in rupees per trip / S is a capacity shift
01

Hotels & stays

Hotel rooms and accommodation stock set a practical ceiling on overnight visitor volume.

02

Transport

Rail, roads, flights and last-mile access determine how reliably visitors can arrive and circulate.

03

Homestays

Local lodging adds distributed capacity and can keep more tourism income in neighborhoods.

04

Assets & services

Tangible amenities, clean public spaces, guides, safety and digital booking all affect usable supply.

At the base case, Qd = 30 - 0.0025P and Qs = 5 + 0.00375P. Their intersection is ₹4,000 and 20 lakh trips. Point elasticity is (dQd/dP) x (P/Qd); its absolute value changes as price changes.

The demand/supply equations, shift sizes, and Jaipur CVP figures are hypothetical teaching assumptions. They show direction and sensitivity, not measured city-level forecasts. A higher short-run supply price response here simplifies real room and transport constraints.

Methods / reading list

Read the
fine print.

This is an educational interactive report, not an official tourism statistical release. The 2021-22 GDP values, visitor-spending split, Jaipur seasonality index, CVP inputs, jobs proxy and market curves are illustrative. The survey's default records are synthetic, never collected from tourists. The 2023-25 national benchmarks are rounded and should be checked against the latest source tables before formal submission.

For a completed field study, collect 20 genuine, consented responses in your own Google Form, replace the demonstration chart through CSV import, and cite the date and sampling method. Keep GDP value added, visitor spending, arrivals and model revenue as separate concepts.
