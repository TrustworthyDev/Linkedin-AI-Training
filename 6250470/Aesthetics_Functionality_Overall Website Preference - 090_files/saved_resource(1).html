A CLOSER LOOK AT CIRCULATION

A small passage.
An important difference.

Arteries carry oxygen-rich blood away from your heart. Their inner passage, called the lumen, helps determine how easily blood can move.

When the passage is clear
A smooth, healthy artery wall surrounds a wide lumen. Blood flows with less resistance, helping it reach the tissues that need it.
When plaque builds up
Fatty deposits can narrow the lumen. Blood faces more resistance, pressure can rise upstream, and flow to tissues downstream may decrease. The heart may have to work harder to maintain circulation.

This diagram is a conceptual illustration, not a measurement of blood pressure or a diagnostic tool. The faster-moving cells show flow through the local narrowing, not an increase in total blood flow. For personal medical advice, speak with a qualified healthcare professional.
